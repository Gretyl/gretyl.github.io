Annual US nonfinancial corporate net equity issuance from 1975 to 2025, deflated to 2025 dollars — a half-century view of the structural shift from net issuance to persistent net retirement after the 1982 adoption of SEC Rule 10b-18. Drawing on the Federal Reserve's Z.1 Financial Accounts series NCBCEBA027N and BLS CPI-U deflators, the chart annotates three inflection points: the 1982 Rule 10b-18 safe harbor, the 1994 start of 32 consecutive years of net retirement, and the 2007 real-terms trough of −$838B (2025 dollars). Cumulative 1975–2025 net retirement totals −$12.4T in 2025 dollars (up from −$10.4T nominal), and the 1988 LBO-wave year (−$351B real) was larger than most post-2010 years on an inflation-adjusted basis.

**Key findings:**
- The 1982–1994 transition, not the 1990s buyback boom, is the structural break.
- Real-terms deflation reveals the late-1980s LBO wave as comparable in magnitude to the post-GFC repurchase era.
- Net retirement has continued uninterrupted through every recession and policy regime since 1994.
