Top-1% U.S. household wealth share across five independent methodologies, 1985–2025 — a median-and-envelope view of four decades of concentration. The bold median line rises from roughly 24% to 33% across four marketable-wealth measures (DFA, PSZ, SZZ, KSS), while a fifth methodology (CMS) that includes Social Security entitlements tells the same directional story at a lower altitude. Three structural breaks are annotated: the 1995 financialization regime, the 2008–10 GFC pause, and the post-2017 TCJA-plus-pandemic surge that pushed the PSZ estimate to 38.5% by 2022.

**Key findings:**
- Five methodologies disagree on the level but agree on the direction — the question is *how much*, never *whether*.
- The GFC temporarily reduced mark-to-market concentration (DFA) but left capitalized-income concentration (PSZ) essentially unchanged.
- Post-2016 the four-dataset median is no longer well-defined; DFA and PSZ continue individually, diverging from 28.5% to 38.5%.
