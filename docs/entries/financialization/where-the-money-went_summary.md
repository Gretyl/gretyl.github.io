Two Sankey diagrams tracing U.S. nonfinancial corporate cash flow through its competing uses in 1982 and 2024, in real 2025 dollars — the plumbing of shareholder primacy. The 2024 corporate sector handles 3.8 times the inflation-adjusted dollar volume of 1982, but the morphology of the flow has changed: the capex channel shrinks as a share of the whole, the shareholder-return channels (dividends and buybacks) engorge, and a second inflow — borrowed money — appears large enough to fund most of what flows out to shareholders.

**Key findings:**
- In 1982 buybacks were statistically zero and net equity flow was slightly positive; by 2024 net buybacks alone reached $415B (real).
- Borrowed money became a parallel inflow, with $815B in net new debt issuance supplementing $4.21T in operating cash flow.
- Capital expenditure grew in absolute terms ($3.00T real) but shrank as a share of total corporate cash allocation.
